# Culture Key — Working With Organizations

Culture Key is a human-governance framework for AI systems.

It helps organizations examine whether governance can remain meaningful
as AI capability, autonomy, integration, scale, or real-world impact increases.

The focus is not governance as documentation.

It is whether authority, accountability, intervention, recovery, review,
and correction can still operate when they are actually needed.

---

## The Governance Problem

AI capability can expand faster than the governance structures surrounding it.

As systems become more capable or consequential, organizations may face:

- unclear authority over consequential decisions
- accountability gaps across teams or systems
- governance processes that exist in policy but fail operationally
- intervention mechanisms that are too slow to remain meaningful
- weak rollback or recovery capability
- unresolved conflicts between legitimate governance principles
- insufficient decision traceability
- governance assumptions that have not been revalidated after system change

Culture Key helps make these weaknesses visible before increased impact
makes them harder to correct.

---

## How Culture Key Can Be Applied

### Pre-Scale Governance Audit

A structured review of governance readiness before wider deployment,
major capability expansion, increased autonomy, new integrations,
or greater real-world impact.

The audit may examine:

- authority and accountability
- risk and evidence evaluation
- state and decision logic
- escalation
- intervention and stop capability
- rollback and recovery
- arbitration
- traceability
- review and revalidation

The purpose is not to certify that a system is safe.

It is to determine whether the governance functions relevant to the
proposed operating condition are defined, legitimate, and operationally
credible.

---

### STOP Framework

The Culture Key STOP Framework examines four capabilities that become
critical when AI systems approach consequential deployment or expansion:

- Authority
- Veto Velocity
- Rollback Integrity
- Arbitration

STOP asks whether meaningful intervention remains possible when normal
governance assumptions fail or conflict.

It can be used as a specialized pre-scale governance lens within a broader
Culture Key review.

---

### Governance Mapping and Advisory

Organizations may also use Culture Key to examine specific governance
questions, including:

- authority and accountability boundaries
- escalation and intervention design
- governance failure modes
- decision traceability
- consent and operational boundaries
- governance drift
- change control and revalidation
- conflicts requiring legitimate arbitration

The scope should be defined by the governance problem being examined,
rather than by a fixed implementation template.

---

## How an Engagement Works

A Culture Key engagement may include:

1. defining the system and governance question
2. identifying the relevant operating context and boundaries
3. mapping applicable governance functions
4. reviewing available evidence
5. identifying gaps, conflicts, or weak intervention points
6. documenting findings and unresolved questions
7. recommending targeted governance actions
8. supporting revalidation where material changes are made

The required level of access depends on the question being examined.

Sensitive technical or organizational information should not be collected
unless it is necessary for the agreed governance scope.

---

## What Culture Key Does Not Do

Culture Key does not:

- certify overall AI safety
- guarantee legal or regulatory compliance
- replace cybersecurity or technical safety engineering
- provide autonomous governance authority
- prescribe a universal technical architecture
- remove responsibility from the organization deploying the system

Governance decisions remain the responsibility of actors with legitimate
authority and appropriate competence.

---

## Who It May Be Useful For

Culture Key may be relevant to:

- AI startups approaching wider deployment
- product teams integrating AI or LLM systems
- responsible AI and governance teams
- safety teams
- organizations deploying high-agency or multi-component AI systems
- decision-makers responsible for consequential AI expansion

---

## Working Principle

> Increasing technical capability should not exceed the demonstrated
> capacity for meaningful governance.

Culture Key is designed to help organizations examine that capacity
before the cost of correction becomes greater than the room to act.
