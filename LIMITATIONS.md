# ⚠️ Culture Key — Limitations (Public)

Culture Key is a human-governance framework for AI systems designed to support
governability across the AI lifecycle.

It provides governance structures, questions, controls, and decision
boundaries.

It does not guarantee that an AI system is safe, lawful, ethical,
secure, or suitable for deployment.

---

## Not a Substitute for Organizational Responsibility

Culture Key does not transfer responsibility from the organizations
and actors that design, deploy, operate, or govern AI systems.

Using the framework does not itself demonstrate responsible governance.

Organizations remain responsible for establishing legitimate authority,
accountability, competent oversight, and appropriate controls for their
specific context.

---

## Not a Legal or Regulatory Compliance Guarantee

Culture Key is not legal advice and does not certify compliance with
applicable laws, regulations, standards, or sector-specific obligations.

Legal and regulatory requirements vary across jurisdictions and domains.

Where such requirements apply, they should be incorporated into the
relevant governance structure by actors with appropriate competence
and authority.

---

## Not a Complete Technical Safety or Security System

Culture Key addresses governance-relevant safety and security structures.

It does not replace:

- cybersecurity engineering
- infrastructure security
- model safety engineering
- access-control systems
- incident-response infrastructure
- technical monitoring and testing
- domain-specific safety controls

Governance controls and technical controls should complement each other.

---

## Not Fully Autonomous Governance

Culture Key does not assume that governance can be delegated entirely
to automated systems.

Automated mechanisms may detect, evaluate, restrict, or intervene within
explicitly authorized boundaries.

High-impact, contested, novel, or governance-critical decisions may require
review or escalation to an authority with the legitimate mandate to act.

Automation does not create its own authority.

---

## Not a Universal Implementation Architecture

Culture Key defines governance functions and relationships.

It does not require a specific:

- AI model
- agent architecture
- orchestration system
- technical platform
- organizational structure
- implementation stack

Reference agents and architectures used by Culture Key illustrate possible
implementations.

They are not mandatory components of the framework.

---

## Context Matters

Governance requirements vary according to:

- system capability
- intended use
- affected people and rights
- operational scale
- reversibility
- propagation speed
- risk domain
- organizational capacity
- legal and institutional context

Culture Key should therefore be adapted without silently weakening its
governance purpose or protected boundaries.

---

## Governance Cannot Eliminate Uncertainty

No governance framework can identify every future failure, interaction,
misuse pattern, or emergent risk.

Culture Key is designed to improve the ability to detect, question,
intervene, escalate, recover, and learn.

It does not eliminate uncertainty.

---

## Evidence and Validation

A documented governance structure is not evidence that the structure works
in practice.

Claims about governability should be supported, where appropriate, by
testing, operational evidence, traceability, review, and revalidation.

Framework adoption should not be confused with demonstrated effectiveness.

---

## Public Repository Scope

This public repository documents Culture Key governance concepts,
architecture, frameworks, and public reference material.

It does not necessarily expose:

- production infrastructure
- private enforcement logic
- operational thresholds
- security-sensitive controls
- credentials or access mechanisms
- organization-specific deployments
- internal safety tooling

Transparency should support accountability without requiring disclosure
that would materially weaken security.

---

## Evolution of the Framework

Culture Key is an evolving governance framework.

Its assumptions, mechanisms, metrics, and controls should remain open
to evidence-based revision.

Changes that materially affect governance meaning, authority, or protected
boundaries should follow the applicable versioning and review process.

Evolution should improve governability without allowing change to become
an untraceable escape from accountability.

---

## Final Principle

Culture Key can provide governance structure.

It cannot provide governance commitment.

**A framework becomes meaningful only when its principles, authority boundaries,
controls, and accountability mechanisms are implemented and tested in practice.**
