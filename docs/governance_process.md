# 🧭 Culture Key — Governance Process

This document describes how the Culture Key governance framework itself
may evolve over time.

A framework concerned with legitimate authority, accountability, review,
and correction must apply those requirements to its own evolution.

---

## Governance Philosophy

Culture Key treats governance as a living structure.

Its current architecture is not assumed to remain valid simply because it
has previously been accepted.

New evidence, implementation experience, changed operating conditions,
governance failure, or demonstrated drift may reveal that an existing
assumption requires review.

Changes to the framework must remain:

- authorized
- transparent
- traceable
- reviewable
- open to reasoned challenge

Adaptability does not mean uncontrolled change.

It means that correction remains possible through a legitimate governance
process.

---

## Proposing Changes

Proposed changes may concern:

- core governance functions
- authority or accountability boundaries
- risk and evidence evaluation
- state and decision logic
- intervention or recovery mechanisms
- escalation or arbitration
- traceability and review requirements
- specialized frameworks or practical applications

Proposals should identify:

1. what is being changed
2. why the change is needed
3. what evidence or reasoning supports it
4. which parts of the framework may be affected
5. whether revalidation of related material is required

Public changes may be documented through pull requests, issues, or other
reviewable governance records.

---

## Review Process

Framework changes should be evaluated for:

- alignment with foundational governance principles
- effect on legitimate human authority
- effect on accountability and traceability
- operational consequences
- new risks or failure modes introduced by the change
- consistency with other parts of the framework
- reversibility or recovery implications where relevant

Major structural changes should include explicit reasoning.

Approval of a change does not make it permanently exempt from future review.

---

## Decision and Change Traceability

Material governance changes should preserve a reconstructable record of:

- the proposal
- relevant evidence
- the authority under which the change was reviewed
- significant objections or unresolved questions
- the resulting decision
- the version or material affected

The purpose is not documentation for its own sake.

It is to preserve accountability and make later review possible.

---

## Revalidation

A governance change may affect assumptions elsewhere in the framework.

When that happens, related material should be reviewed and, where necessary,
revalidated.

The framework should not silently accumulate contradictions between its
principles, specialized frameworks, practical applications, and public
documentation.

---

## Principle

> Governance that cannot examine and correct itself eventually becomes
> governance by inheritance.

Culture Key is designed to evolve without treating change itself as
self-authorizing.
