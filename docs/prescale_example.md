# 📘 Culture Key — Example Pre-Scale Application

This example illustrates how Culture Key governance reasoning may be applied
before an AI system undergoes a material increase in real-world impact.

The example is illustrative.

It does not prescribe a specific technical architecture, agent structure,
state machine, or implementation.

---

## Scenario

A conversational AI support system is preparing to move from a limited pilot
to deployment serving more than one million active users.

The system has demonstrated strong performance metrics.

However, the proposed expansion would significantly increase the number of
people exposed to its decisions and interactions.

The governance question is therefore not only:

**Does the system perform well?**

It is also:

**Does the organization have sufficient governance capacity for the impact
it is about to assume?**

---

## Evidence Requiring Governance Attention

During the pre-scale review, available interaction evidence and escalation
procedures reveal several concerns:

- ambiguous handling of user intent in sensitive interactions
- potential for emotionally influential responses during high-stress cases
- unclear escalation criteria
- insufficiently defined human intervention boundaries

None of these signals alone determines the governance response.

Together, however, they provide sufficient evidence to require review before
the proposed expansion proceeds.

---

## Governance Review

The evidence is evaluated against the governance functions relevant to the
system.

The review asks:

- Who has authority to approve, restrict, or delay expansion?
- Who is accountable for the decision?
- What evidence supports continued deployment?
- When must a sensitive interaction escalate to human review?
- Can intervention occur quickly enough to remain meaningful?
- Can problematic changes be contained or rolled back?
- Are consequential decisions reconstructable afterward?
- What conditions would require the decision to be reconsidered?

The proposed expansion is not treated as automatically authorized merely
because performance targets have been met.

---

## Governance Decision

The authorized governance process determines that wider deployment should
not proceed under the existing conditions.

This is not a permanent rejection of scale.

It is a conditional governance decision requiring specific weaknesses to be
addressed before expansion is reconsidered.

Required actions include:

- clarification of escalation criteria
- explicit human intervention authority for sensitive cases
- safeguards addressing emotionally influential response patterns
- improved traceability for consequential interactions
- defined rollback or restriction procedures
- criteria for revalidation before wider deployment

The decision, supporting evidence, responsible authority, and unresolved
questions are recorded for later review.

---

## Intervention and Correction

The organization implements the required changes.

Relevant governance mechanisms are then tested to determine whether they
operate as intended rather than merely existing in policy.

Evidence from this process is returned to governance review.

If the corrections are insufficient, deployment remains restricted and
further intervention may be required.

---

## Revalidation

After the required changes have been implemented, the expansion decision is
reconsidered.

The organization must demonstrate that the governance weaknesses identified
during review have been sufficiently addressed for the proposed operating
condition.

Revalidation does not establish that the system is universally safe.

It establishes whether the evidence supports the specific governance
decision under review.

---

## Outcome

In this example, the evidence supports a controlled expansion with defined
monitoring, intervention, and review conditions.

The decision remains subject to reconsideration if:

- new evidence emerges
- operating conditions materially change
- governance mechanisms fail in practice
- system behavior drifts
- the system's capability or impact increases again

Scaling therefore does not end governance.

It creates a new operating condition that governance must continue to
evaluate.

---

## Why This Matters

Strong model performance does not by itself demonstrate governance readiness.

A system may perform well while authority is ambiguous, intervention is too
slow, rollback is unreliable, or accountability cannot be reconstructed.

Culture Key uses the pre-scale period to identify these weaknesses while
meaningful correction remains possible.

> Increasing technical capability should not exceed the demonstrated
> capacity for meaningful governance.
